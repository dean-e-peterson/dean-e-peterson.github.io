Responsibility is Different from Guilt

Imagine the following scenario. My grandfather and your grandfather owned a business together. The business thrived. After a falling out, my grandfather cheated your grandfather out of his half of the business.

The business continued to thrive, with my grandfather owning the whole thing. My grandfather's family bought a nice, big house in the nice part of town. My father grew up in a pretty stable environment. He went to college, got a good job, and prospered.

When I came along, I grew up well off, in a good part of town, with a good school. I saw education and success as attainable and even expected. I eventually went to work for my grandfather's business, and am doing well financially. Life has not been a bed of roses. In addition to working hard, I had to deal with family medical problems, grief at the loss of loved ones, and struggles my children sometimes had. Nevertheless, my family is stable, fairly well off, and emotionally somewhat functional.

Your grandfather, after he was cheated out of his half of the business by my grandfather, struggled. He had invested everything in the business. After losing it, he had to get a low paying job at hard labor. He died relatively young, possibly due to the poor conditions he worked in.

Your father grew up poor. Your grandfather died before he could help your father through a tough time, and your father fell into the bottle. He was a good man, tried to fight the addiction, and sometimes was sober and stable for a while, but sometimes he relapsed.

You also grew up poor, in a poor house on the wrong side of town. Your home had love, but also dysfunction caused by your father's addiction and your parents fighting about money. Getting an education was a pipe dream. You did not see being successful as a real possiblity, as you saw few people near you being successful. You have tried to survive on low paying jobs with a broken down house with car. When your child got sick, you had no money to deal with it. The stress has led to you fighting more with your wife. Your children are watching this. They are attending the same poor school you did. They are not so much learning how to strive and thrive, but how to endure.

I recently met you, and learned about the history of our families. I didn't want to believe it, but I looked into it, and found out that my grandfather did indeed cheat yours.

Two Questions

Now, two questions:

  • Are am I to blame for your poor situation?

  • Do I have a responsibility to help you?

The answer to the first question is no. However, I would argue that the answer to the second question is yes.

Why? I did not cheat you. I did not cheat your grandfather However, the better life that I am living is partly built on ill-gotten gains. I may not have stolen them, but I am still benefiting from them all these years later. Meanwhile you and your family are still suffering from the results of that injustice.

Just because you are not to blame, just because you are not guilty, does not mean you do not have a responsibility. If your ancestors were on the wrong side of an historical injustice, you do not need to feel guilty for that. However, you should learn about the long-term effects of that injustice, and consider if you can do anything to help with those long-term effects. You may not be guilty. But you can still be responsible.
